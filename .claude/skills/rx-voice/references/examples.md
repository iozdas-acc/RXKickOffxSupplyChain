# RX Voice — before / after examples

Calibration examples. Each shows a typical "generic consulting" original, the RX rewrite, and the patterns applied. Use these to check your own rewrites.

---

## Example 1 — Hero headline

**Before:**
> "We help our clients leverage AI to unlock transformative business outcomes."

**After:**
> *We pioneer AI transformation with conviction, velocity, and integrity.*
>
> We don't wait for permission. We don't optimise the status quo. We don't ship theatre.

**Patterns applied:** Verb-outcome triple modifier ("conviction, velocity, integrity") + triple negation. Banned words killed: *leverage · unlock · transformative outcomes*.

---

## Example 2 — Service description card

**Before:**
> **Cross-Functional Delivery**
>
> Our comprehensive, end-to-end delivery approach combines strategy, design, and engineering to deliver seamless outcomes for our stakeholders.

**After:**
> **Embedded Ownership**
>
> *We sit with the problem and own the result.*
>
> - Work as small, cross-functional squads embedded where the pain is
> - No hand-offs: the same team discovers, builds, deploys, and iterates adoption
>
> **Ask:** Who really owns this outcome — and is it us?

**Patterns applied:** Title renamed to name the principle, not the service. Italic subtitle added. Verb-led bullets replacing adjective soup. Ask-probe closes. Banned words killed: *comprehensive · end-to-end · seamless · stakeholders*.

---

## Example 3 — CTA button

**Before:**
> "Learn more about our transformation journey"

**After:**
> "Start the mission"

**Patterns applied:** Stakes-first framing. "Mission" replaces "journey." Six words → three. The word "journey" is banned.

---

## Example 4 — Section intro paragraph

**Before:**
> "In today's fast-paced world, AI is changing how businesses operate. We help our clients navigate this complex landscape by delivering innovative, cutting-edge solutions that drive measurable results."

**After:**
> We realise how difficult the AI revolution is going to be for our customers; we resolve to meet that challenge head on. We prove this by taking the hardest problems and wielding the new capabilities to drive that revolution.
>
> We own the outcome. We demand the step change. We compound the win.

**Patterns applied:** Stakes named ("how difficult…"). Verb-outcome triple closes the paragraph. Banned words killed: *in today's fast-paced world · complex landscape · innovative · cutting-edge · drive measurable results*.

---

## Example 5 — Metric / proof point

**Before:**
> "Our solutions drove a 40% improvement in operational efficiency."

**After:**
> "We cut processing time 40%. Same team. Same month. Same prototype, in production."

**Patterns applied:** Triple of specifics. "Improvement" → concrete action ("cut processing time"). "Operational efficiency" — banned abstraction — replaced by a measurable thing. Evidence before claim.

---

## Example 6 — Principle statement (common misfire)

**BAD rewrite** (sounds RX-ish but fails calibration):
> *We are bold, pioneering leaders in transformative AI innovation.*

**Why this fails:**
- Adjective stack (*bold, pioneering*) — violates pattern rules.
- *"transformative AI innovation"* — three banned-adjacent words chained.
- States a claim about themselves, not a principle or stake.
- No concrete verb. "Are" is not a verb that belongs in RX voice.

**GOOD rewrite:**
> *We move first when the path is unclear.*
>
> Momentum over certainty. Unproven is an invitation, not a warning.
>
> **Ask:** If not us, then who? If not now, then when?

**Patterns applied:** Italic principle stated as action. Short-sentence body with "instead of" choice (*momentum over certainty*). Canonical Ask-probe.

---

## Example 7 — Failure mode: losing the voice mid-paragraph

**Partial rewrite that drifts:**
> We don't wait for permission. We don't optimise the status quo. We don't ship theatre. Our dedicated team works closely with stakeholders to leverage best-in-class frameworks that deliver impactful results across the organisation.

**Why this fails:** First three sentences nail the triple negation. Then the writer loses nerve and reverts to agency pitch. All banned vocabulary lights up. The second half undoes the first.

**Fix:** Continue the rhythm or stop.
> We don't wait for permission. We don't optimise the status quo. We don't ship theatre.
>
> We own the outcome. We demand the step change. We compound the win.

**Rule:** Once you've set a triple rhythm, the next sentence either extends it or ends the block. Never drop into soft agency mode.

## Calibration cheat sheet

For every rewrite, run through these checks:

- [ ] First sentence would be false for a McKinsey deck? (Good.)
- [ ] At least one triple somewhere in the block?
- [ ] Italic subtitle present if this is a card?
- [ ] Ask-probe present if this is a card?
- [ ] Zero banned words (lexicon.md)?
- [ ] First-person plural throughout?
- [ ] No exclamation points?
- [ ] Adjective count per sentence ≤ 1?

If any box is unchecked, rewrite it.
