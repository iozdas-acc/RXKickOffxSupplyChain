# RX Voice — recurring patterns

Extract-and-reuse patterns from the manifesto. When rewriting, pick the pattern that fits the source's intent, not the one that sounds coolest.

---

## Pattern 1 — Triple negation

**Shape:** `We don't X. We don't Y. We don't Z.`

**Canonical example:**
> We don't wait for permission. We don't optimise the status quo. We don't ship theatre.

**When to use:** When the original copy is hedged, apologetic, or soft. Use triple negation to draw the line between what RX does and doesn't do.

**When not to use:** When the message is fundamentally positive. Don't force negation onto a principle statement.

---

## Pattern 2 — Verb-outcome triple

**Shape:** `We [verb] the [outcome]. We [verb] the [outcome]. We [verb] the [outcome].`

**Canonical example:**
> We own the outcome. We demand the step change. We compound the win.

**When to use:** When you're stating what RX commits to. These are claims, not negations. Each verb-outcome must be distinct — don't repeat.

**Variants observed:**
- *We pioneer AI transformation with conviction, velocity, and integrity.* (single line, triple modifier)
- *Discovers. Builds. Deploys.* (pure-verb triple, shortest form)

---

## Pattern 3 — Italic principle subtitle

**Shape:** `*We [verb] [object].*` — one sentence, italicised, placed directly under a card title.

**Canonical examples:**
> *We challenge consensus — breakthrough insights are beyond the "best practices."*
> *We sit with the problem and own the result.*
> *We optimise for outcomes, not outputs.*
> *We create the path; we don't wait for someone else to clear it.*

**When to use:** Every card gets one. The subtitle restates the principle as a fact. It is not a tagline — it is a declaration.

**Construction rule:** `We [verb, present tense] [object] [optional em-dash-free elaboration].` Keep it under 15 words.

---

## Pattern 4 — Verb-led bullet

**Shape:** Bullet starts with an imperative-style verb, stated in the group-we form.

**Canonical examples:**
> - Reject the comfort of "others think" and "this has not been proven"
> - Champion independent, unconventional viewpoints that break the norm
> - Work as small, cross-functional squads embedded where the pain is
> - Move first when the path is unclear — momentum over certainty

**Rules:**
- First word is always a verb.
- No adjective stacking. "Bold, pioneering, transformative" → pick one, or preferably drop all three.
- Concrete behaviours. "Work as cross-functional squads where the pain is" beats "Collaborate closely with clients."
- 8-18 words per bullet. Shorter = sharper.

---

## Pattern 5 — Ask: probe

**Shape:** `**Ask:** [one question, directed at the reader].`

**Canonical examples:**
> **Ask:** Are we solving the actual problem or the comfortable one?
> **Ask:** Who really owns this outcome — and is it us?
> **Ask:** If not us, then who? If not now, then when?
> **Ask:** What's the smallest thing we can ship in weeks, not months?

**Rules:**
- Every card ends with an Ask.
- It is a test the reader has to pass. Not rhetorical. Not flattering.
- First-person plural ("are we…", "who really owns…"). Never "are you."
- One question mark per Ask. Don't stack three.
- Exception: "If not us, then who? If not now, then when?" is a two-beat that acts as one probe — use sparingly.

---

## Pattern 6 — Anti-cliché inversion

**Shape:** Take a corporate cliché and invert its assumption.

**Canonical examples from the manifesto:**
- "best practices" → *beyond the "best practices"* (rejecting them, not aspiring to them)
- "unproven" → *treat "unproven" as an invitation, not a warning*
- "conflict" → *treat conflict as a resource, not a risk*
- "bubble" → *proud of the bubble* (reclaiming a pejorative)

**When to use:** When you catch yourself writing a phrase that sounds like it came from a McKinsey deck. Invert it. Make it specific.

---

## Pattern 7 — Stakes-first framing

**Shape:** Lead with what's at stake, not with what RX does.

**Canonical example:**
> We exist to make inaction untenable.

**When to use:** For headline / hero copy. Don't start with "We are RX, we help clients…" — start with the world as it is, the stake you're picking up.

---

## Pattern 8 — The "instead of" choice

**Shape:** `[sharp action], not [soft alternative]`

**Canonical examples:**
> outcomes, not outputs
> momentum over certainty
> structured disagreement wins
> weeks, not months

**When to use:** Whenever the reader could mistake RX's position for the conventional one. Force the contrast.

---

## Punctuation rules (enforced)

- **Full stops.** Short sentences. Periods do the work.
- **Em dashes ( — ) are allowed, but sparingly.** The manifesto uses them to append clarification, not to break sentences. One em dash per paragraph max.
- **Semicolons exist but are rare.** *"We create the path; we don't wait…"* — used when the two halves are causally linked.
- **No exclamation points.** Ever. RX doesn't shout.
- **Quote marks for reclaimed terms.** *"best practices"*, *"bubble"*, *"unproven"* — marks them as terms being interrogated.
- **Italic for subtitles and probes only.** Don't italicise for emphasis mid-sentence.
