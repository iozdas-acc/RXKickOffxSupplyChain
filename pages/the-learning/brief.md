# What We Learned — Brief

## Purpose

The honest reckoning. Name the structural tension the 12-week engagement revealed: RX moves faster than clients can absorb. 12 weeks at full RX pace was too costly. The client needed time to adopt, digest, and buy in. This is a structural problem — not a client failing, not a delivery failure. This page's job is to make that insight feel credible, not defensive.

## Audience

Stakeholders who just saw strong outcomes in Chapter 2. They may sense what's coming — the "but." This page earns their trust by saying it plainly before they have to ask.

## Success criteria

1. Visitor recognises the pace mismatch as a real structural insight — not a spin.
2. Visitor understands specifically why the 12-week model was too costly (time, resource, pace relative to client adoption).
3. Visitor is set up to receive a structural fix — not a patch — in Chapter 4.

## Sections / content blocks

1. **Chapter indicator** — "03 / 05"
2. **Section headline** — "What We Learned" (or a sharper tension statement)
3. **The insight** — two-part structure:
   - Part A: "RX can go fast." (one line — don't dwell)
   - Part B: "The client needs time to adopt." (the real point — expand here)
4. **The cost** — 12 weeks at full RX pace: too costly. Specific: cost to client, resource intensity, pace mismatch observable in adoption lag.
5. **The framing** — this is not a client problem. This is a model problem. The engagement proved RX's capability and revealed a delivery design gap.
6. **Transition** — "So we redesigned the model." → advance to Chapter 4.

## Copy tone

Direct. Unhedged. This page must not read like a post-mortem apology or a reframe. It reads like a company that ran an experiment, looked at the data, and drew a clear conclusion. Short sentences. No softening.

One rule: never use "challenge" as a euphemism here. Say what the problem was.

## Hard constraints

- Do not frame this as a client shortcoming — the learning is about RX's model design
- Must set up Chapter 4 without pre-empting it — introduce the tension, not the solution
- Avoid generic consulting retrospective language ("we identified key learnings", "we iterated")
