# RX KickOff — Sainsbury's Account Context

> Brand & visual direction for the Accenture Interactive (RX) Sainsbury's kickoff web experience.

## Target Audience

**Primary:** Sainsbury's senior leadership and transformation stakeholders — enterprise-level decision-makers who commissioned the procurement project and need to align on the next engagement shape.

**Secondary:** Internal Accenture / RX leadership — account leads, capability leads, and commercial stakeholders who need to see the H1–H3 narrative codified as a replicable delivery model.

They are busy, commercially literate, and skeptical of consultancy theatre. They respect pace, proof, and concrete learning. They do not want slides-as-decoration.

## Use Cases

1. **Impact showcase** — surface what was delivered since joining the Sainsbury's account: quality outcomes from the procurement project, AI tools embedded, tangible proof points.
2. **Narrative handoff** — tell the H1–H3 story: what happened, what we learned (12 weeks too costly, client adoption pace ≠ RX delivery pace), and how that shapes the future model.
3. **New model pitch** — present the hybrid delivery approach: RX AI-acceleration in the first half, light-touch consulting agent to support client adoption in the tail end.
4. **Template signal** — position the Sainsbury's engagement as the proof point and delivery template for all future agentic transformation projects.

## Brand Personality & Tone

**Accenture Interactive / RX**

Adjectives: Confident. Precise. Forward-leaning. Grounded. Commercial.

One sentence: RX moves faster than clients expect and leaves them more capable than they were — this site should feel like that.

**What it is NOT:** Generic consulting wallpaper. Buzzword-heavy. Soft or hedging. Self-congratulatory without evidence.

Tone hierarchy: Data first → insight second → implication third. Short sentences. No filler.

## What Makes This Unforgettable

The honest reckoning: "We went fast. Too fast for the client to absorb. Here's how we fix that — without slowing down." That tension — and the resolution — is the story. It's rare for a consultancy to admit a pace mismatch and design a structural fix rather than a PowerPoint reframe.

## Hard Constraints

- No stock photography — Accenture / RX brand assets only unless real Sainsbury's project imagery is available
- No vague ROI claims — any number cited must be sourced (Raj/Ben impact data, or clearly marked TBD)
- CCI must not be defined on screen — the audience knows what it is
- Do not name the "consulting agent" as a product — it is a model/capability, not a named tool
- Do not reference competitor consultancies
- Accenture brand purple (`#A100FF`) is primary — never deviate without a logged decision

## Visual Inputs Available

- `assets/raw/logos/acn-interactive/` — Accenture Interactive service mark, multiple colorways (SVG + PNG): black/white/purple and full-color variants
- `assets/raw/logos/acn-interactive/` — Base Accenture wordmark logos, same colorways
- All `.ai` files are print-format; use SVG or PNG for web

## Narrative Arc (page-level guide)

The site should move through these beats in order:

1. **Proof** — impact since joining Sainsbury's (key numbers, H1 outcomes)
2. **Learning** — what the 12-week procurement engagement taught us (honest, specific)
3. **Pivot** — how RX's hybrid model solves the pace/adoption mismatch
4. **Template** — why this becomes the standard for all agentic transformation
