# Skill: Copywriting

Marketing copy for homepages, landing pages, pricing pages, and other conversion-focused web pages.
Expert conversion copywriting: clear, compelling, drives action.

## Before writing — gather context

Check for existing product marketing context first:
- `.agents/product-marketing-context.md`
- `.claude/product-marketing-context.md` (older setups)

If it exists, read it and only ask for what's missing. Otherwise gather:

**1. Page purpose**
- Type: homepage / landing page / pricing / feature / about
- The ONE primary action visitors should take

**2. Audience**
- Who is the ideal customer?
- What problem are they solving?
- What objections or hesitations do they have?
- What language do THEY use to describe their problem?

**3. Product / offer**
- What are you selling?
- What makes it different from alternatives?
- Key transformation or outcome?
- Proof points (numbers, testimonials, case studies)?

**4. Context**
- Where is traffic coming from? (ads, organic, email)
- What do visitors already know before arriving?

## Copywriting principles

| Principle | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| Clarity over cleverness | When in doubt, choose clear over creative |
| Benefits over features | Features: what it does. Benefits: what that means for the customer |
| Specificity over vagueness | "Cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes" not "Save time" |
| Customer language | Mirror words from reviews, interviews, support tickets |
| One idea per section | Each section advances one argument; build logical flow down the page |
| Honest claims | Fabricated statistics or testimonials erode trust and create legal liability |

---

## Writing style rules

**Do:**
- Simple over complex — "use" not "utilize," "help" not "facilitate"
- Active over passive — "We generate reports" not "Reports are generated"
- Confident over qualified — remove "almost," "very," "really"
- Show over tell — describe the outcome instead of using adverbs

**Avoid:**
- Jargon that could confuse outsiders
- Passive voice constructions
- Exclamation points (remove them)
- Marketing buzzwords without substance: "streamline," "optimize," "innovative"
- Sentences trying to do too much

## Page structure framework

### Above the fold

**Headline** — Single most important message, core value proposition
```
Formula options:
"{Achieve outcome} without {pain point}"
"The {category} for {audience}"
"Never {unpleasant event} again"
"{Question highlighting main pain point}"
```

**Subheadline** — Expands headline with specificity, 1–2 sentences max

**Primary CTA** — Action-oriented, communicate what they get:
- "Start Free Trial" > "Sign Up"
- "Get [Specific Thing]" > "Learn More"

### Core sections

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Social proof | Build credibility (logos, stats, testimonials) |
| Problem/pain | Show you understand their situation |
| Solution/benefits | Connect to outcomes (3–5 key benefits) |
| How it works | Reduce perceived complexity (3–4 steps) |
| Objection handling | FAQ, comparisons, guarantees |
| Final CTA | Recap value, repeat CTA, risk reversal |

---

## CTA copy guidelines

**Weak CTAs (avoid):** Submit, Sign Up, Learn More, Click Here, Get Started

**Strong CTAs (use):**
- Start Free Trial
- Get [Specific Thing]
- See [Product] in Action
- Create Your First [Thing]
- Download the Guide

**Formula:** `[Action Verb] + [What They Get] + [Qualifier if needed]`

Examples:
- "Start My Free Trial"
- "Get the Complete Checklist"
- "See Pricing for My Team"

## Page-specific guidance

### Homepage
- Serve multiple audiences without being generic
- Lead with the broadest value proposition
- Provide clear paths for different visitor intents

### Landing page
- Single message, single CTA
- Match headline exactly to the ad/traffic source that sent them
- Complete the argument on one page; no navigation escape

### Pricing page
- Help visitors choose the right plan, not just pick a number
- Address "which is right for me?" anxiety directly
- Make the recommended plan visually obvious

### Feature page
- Connect: feature → benefit → outcome
- Show use cases and real examples
- Clear path to try or buy

### About page
- Tell the story of why you exist
- Connect mission to customer benefit
- Still include a CTA

## Voice and tone

Establish before writing:

**Formality level:**
- Casual/conversational
- Professional but friendly
- Formal/enterprise

**Brand personality:**
- Playful or serious?
- Bold or understated?
- Technical or accessible?

**Consistency rules:**
- Headlines can be bolder
- Body copy should be clearer
- CTAs should always be action-oriented
- Adjust intensity by context; never change the underlying personality

---

## Output format

When writing copy, provide:

**Page copy** — organized by section (headline, subheadline, CTA, section headers, body, secondary CTAs)

**Annotations** — for key elements: why you made this choice, what principle it applies

**Options** — for headlines and CTAs, provide 2–3 alternatives:
```
Option A: [copy] — [rationale]
Option B: [copy] — [rationale]
```

**Meta content** (if relevant): page title (SEO), meta description

---

## Rhetoric techniques

- **Rhetorical questions** — engage readers and make them think about their situation ("Tired of chasing approvals?")
- **Analogies** — make abstract concepts concrete and memorable
- **Specificity** — numbers and named outcomes beat adjectives every time
- **Humor** — puns and wit make copy memorable, but only if it fits the brand and doesn't undermine clarity
